Defensive Shotgun Shooting

The shotgun for home and property defense: legal limits, setup, ammunition, how buckshot actually patterns at room distances, the manual of arms, keeping the gun fed, recoil control, moving indoors with a long gun, and low light.

Built on common defensive shotgun curricula, manufacturer pattern data, and general U.S. self-defense law. This is training information, not legal advice. Pattern figures are typical for a cylinder-bore 18.5-inch barrel; pattern your own gun with your own load.

01Legal and ethical limits

Deadly force is justified only when you reasonably believe it's necessary to prevent imminent death or serious bodily harm to yourself or another innocent person. Every pellet you fire is your responsibility, including the ones that miss. See the Defensive Handgun page for the full framework.

Ability

The attacker has the means to kill or cause serious injury.

Opportunity

They're in a position to use it now.

Jeopardy

Their actions show intent to use it.

02Role of the shotgun

Strengths

  • Each 00 buckshot shell fires about nine .33-caliber pellets at once. Very effective at stopping a threat at short range.
  • Flexible: buckshot for close range, slugs for precision out to 50–100 yd.
  • Widely legal and familiar; pumps are inexpensive and reliable.
Garand Thumb: Why the military still uses pump shotguns2:20Dirty Civilian: When all you have is a shotgun3:39

Limits

  • Limited capacity (4–8 rounds) and slow to reload.
  • Heavy recoil; harder for smaller shooters without good technique.
  • Long and harder to maneuver indoors than a handgun or short carbine.
  • It still has to be aimed. At room distance the pattern is only a few inches wide.

05Patterns at room distance

The most common misunderstanding about shotguns is that you don't have to aim. Across a bedroom the pattern is about the size of a fist. It grows about an inch per yard with standard buckshot from a cylinder bore, about half that with tight-patterning loads.

Find your gun's maximum buckshot distance: the farthest range where every pellet still lands inside an 8–10 inch circle. Beyond that, use a slug or get closer.

06Manual of arms

Most defensive shotguns are pumps. A pump only works if you run it: all the way back, all the way forward, hard. A short stroke under stress is the pump's most common failure.

Garand Thumb: Pump shotguns: moving and shooting5:40
Inside the action: full stroke vs. short stroke

Pump

  • Run the action as soon as you fire; don't wait to see the result.
  • Support hand stays on the forend; it never has to search for it.
  • To unload: on many pumps, cycle shells out one at a time through the action, finger off the trigger, or depress the shell latch per the manual.

Semi-automatic

  • Softer felt recoil (gas) and no pump to short-stroke.
  • Test with your defensive load; some guns are picky with reduced-recoil loads.
  • Ride the bolt forward fully when chambering; easing it can leave it out of battery.

Storage condition

A common home setup is "cruiser ready": tube full, chamber empty, action closed on an empty chamber, safety off (on guns where that's safe), so one full pump chambers a round. It's drop-safer and lets you decide when to chamber. Follow your gun's manual and keep it locked from unauthorized people.

08The sidearm when the shotgun runs dry

With a shotgun, the handgun is the secondary weapon. A shotgun holds few rounds and reloads one shell at a time, so in a longer fight the sidearm has two jobs.

1 · Fighting back to the long gun

Trouble rarely starts with the shotgun in your hands. The handgun is what you have when you're caught away from it. Use it to survive long enough to reach the shotgun, then switch.

2 · Covering a malfunction or empty gun

When the shotgun runs dry or stops at close range, switching to the sidearm is faster than feeding or clearing the shotgun under pressure. Loading a tube shell by shell is the slowest reload of any common defensive gun, and stress makes it slower.

ProblemTime to fix itTime to switch to the sidearm
Click (empty chamber)1–1.5 s to rack, if a shell is in the tube1.5–2.5 s to the first shot

Typical times for a trained shooter. Stress slows every column, and it slows the fine-motor fixes the most.

When to switch

  • The threat is close and the shotgun is empty or stopped.
  • You're in the open with no time to feed it.
  • More than one shell is needed right now.

When to fix it instead

  • You're behind cover with time to feed the tube.
  • Only one shell is needed: port-load it.
  • Once the threat is stopped or you reach cover: load the shotgun and go back to it.

Making the switch

  • A sling lets the shotgun hang instead of being dropped. Without one, set it down where you can keep track of it.
  • Support hand pushes the shotgun aside; firing hand draws. Don't look at the shotgun.
  • Practice dry with an unloaded shotgun until running empty triggers the draw without thinking.

The sidearm doesn't usually win a fight a shotgun can win. It keeps you in it long enough to get the shotgun loaded again.

09Stance and recoil

  • Stock high in the pocket of the shoulder, inside the shoulder joint, not on the arm.
  • Bring the stock to the cheek; keep the head upright.
  • Run the pump during recoil; the rearward motion of the gun helps you cycle it.

10Moving indoors with a long gun

A shotgun is 36–40 inches long. Leading with the muzzle at corners and doorways lets someone around the corner see it, grab it, or push it aside before you see them. Stay back from corners and keep the gun compressed until you need to shoot.

Approaching a corner with a long gun

Retention

  • Pull the stock under the arm or to the chest in tight spaces (compressed ready).
  • If someone grabs the barrel, pull it toward you while stepping back and rotating the muzzle toward them, or drive the muzzle into them.
  • Keep the sling on. It makes the gun hard to take.

Corners and doors

  • Slice the corner from several feet back, like the CQB Primer shows.
  • Don't search alone if you can avoid it. Hold a position and let police clear.
  • Know your lines of fire relative to family members' rooms.

11Cover and concealment

Cover stops the rounds being fired at you. Concealment only hides you. Both matter, but they aren't interchangeable, and inside a house almost everything is concealment.

Cover

Something solid enough to stop incoming fire: an engine block, a large tree, earth, thick masonry. Fight, reload, and fix malfunctions from behind it. It's temporary: repeated hits wear it down, and the threat can move to a new angle.

Concealment

Something that hides you but won't stop a bullet: drywall, doors, car doors, hedges, furniture, darkness, smoke. You can't be aimed at if you can't be seen, so use it to move, reposition, or break contact. Expect fire through it at where you were last seen.

Using cover with a shotgun

  • Stand back from it. The barrel is long; crowding a doorframe or corner puts the muzzle past the edge where it can be seen or grabbed.
  • Don't hug walls or cars. Rounds that hit a hard surface at a shallow angle skip along it a few inches off the surface.
  • Indoors, a position matters more than a wall. Interior walls stop nothing; choose a spot where the threat has to come to you through a doorway, with something solid behind them.
  • Reload behind cover, not in the open. Feeding a tube takes time, and cover is what buys it.

12Low light

  • A forend light is the best option. Your support hand both runs the pump and controls the light.
  • Identify before you shoot: hands and what's in them.
  • Light on to look, off, move. In a hallway the splash lights you as well.
  • Flash from a short barrel is bright at night. Practice in low light so it isn't a surprise.

14After an incident

  1. Stay in a defensible position and keep watching until you're sure it's over.
  2. Call 911: location, that you were attacked, that someone needs medical help, your description, and that you're armed.
  3. Set the shotgun down before police arrive. Empty hands visible; follow every command.
  4. Give the basic facts, point out evidence and witnesses, then say you'll cooperate fully after speaking with your attorney.
