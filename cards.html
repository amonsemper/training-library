Printable Cards

One-page cards to print, fill in, and keep where you'll need them: in the go-bag, on the fridge, in the glovebox, by the radio. Each one prints on its own page in plain black and white.

Use the Print button on a card to print just that card, or your browser's print command to print all of them (one per page). Fill them in by hand; nothing you write leaves the paper.

01Bleeding control

Make the scene safe. Call 911. Gloves on if you have them. Then work in order:

M · Massive bleedingArm or leg: tourniquet high and tight above the wound; tighten until bleeding stops; write the time. Add a second above the first if needed. Groin, armpit, neck: pack the wound tight with gauze or cloth, then hold hard pressure 3+ minutes.
A · AirwayUnconscious but breathing: roll onto the side (recovery position).
R · RespirationLook for chest wounds; seal any open wound with a chest seal or plastic, taped down.
C · CirculationRecheck your bleeding control. Look for other wounds, including under the body.
H · Hypothermia / headKeep them warm and off the ground. Protect a possible head or spine injury.

Don't loosen a tourniquet once it's on. Don't trade a working press for a loose wrap. Tell the medics what happened, what you found, what you did, and when.

Reference, not training: take a Stop the Bleed class. More on the Field Medical page.

04Water treatment

Cloudy? Let it settle and strain through cloth or a coffee filter first. Possibly chemical (fuel, floodwater)? Don't drink it; nothing at home fixes that.

CheckShould smell faintly of chlorine. If not, dose again and wait 15 more minutes.
Filter + tabletFilter removes bacteria and protozoa; chlorine dioxide tablet adds viruses. Follow the package wait time.

Water in the house: water heater tank (power or gas off first), pipes (main off, open the top faucet, drain the lowest), toilet tank (not the bowl). Plan: 1 gallon per person per day, two weeks.

From CDC and EPA emergency disinfection guidance. More on the Water page.
