Common Practice Drills

Named drills give you a fixed task and a standard, so you can measure progress across months instead of guessing. This page collects the well-known ones for the handgun, rifle, and shotgun, with the setup, round count, standard, and what each one builds. There's a par timer at the top you can run on your phone at the range.

Run every drill on a range that allows it: drawing from a holster, moving, and shooting multiple targets aren't allowed everywhere. Standards are common published figures; where sources differ, the most widely used version is given. Start slow and clean, then speed up. A fast miss isn't progress.

00Par timer

0.00
Press Start, then get ready. A beep after a random delay starts the string; a second beep marks the par time.

The timer uses your phone's speaker. Turn the volume up and wear electronic hearing protection so you can hear it. It doesn't detect shots; it gives a start signal and a par signal.

01How to use drills

  • Shoot it cold. The first run of the day, without warm-up, is the honest measure of what you can do on demand.
    Garand Thumb: The 12-round drill (Chris Way)7:12Dirty Civilian: EDC drills with Kyle Lamb2:50
  • Write it down. Date, gun, ammunition, time, hits, and what went wrong. Progress shows up in the log, not in memory.
  • Penalize misses. Most standards add time for each miss. Don't let a fast time hide poor hits.
  • Change one thing at a time. If you're working on the draw, keep the distance and target the same.

02Handgun drills

Mozambique (failure drill)

Distance3–7 yd
Rounds3
Target8" chest, 3×5" head box
Standard3.0 s from holster at 7 yd; 2.5 s advanced

Two shots to the chest, then one precise shot to the head. Named for an incident in the Mozambique war of independence that firearms instructor Jeff Cooper later taught as a drill. It trains changing from a fast pair to a precise shot, which means slowing down and refining the sight picture for the third round. Most modern classes teach assessing after the pair instead of firing the head shot automatically; run it both ways.

Administrative Results: Modified Mozambique13:29

Dicken drill

Distance40 yd
Rounds10
TargetUSPSA or IDPA torso
StandardFrom concealment, 8 of 10 hits in 15 s

Named after Elisjsha Dicken, who stopped an active shooter at the Greenwood Park Mall in Indiana in July 2022 with a pistol, firing from about 40 yards. It tests whether you can make hits at a distance most carry practice ignores. Some versions score it as a USPSA hit factor or allow a supported position with a time penalty. Shoot it slowly and accurately first; at 40 yards, the trigger press matters more than speed.

Bill drill

Distance7 yd
Rounds6
TargetA-zone or 8" circle
Standard3.0 s good, 2.0 s advanced

Draw and fire six shots as fast as you can keep them all in the A-zone. Named after Bill Wilson. It trains recoil control and seeing the sights lift and return between shots.

Administrative Results: Bill drills, stock vs. custom pistols2:59

FAST (Fundamentals, Accuracy and Speed Test)

Distance7 yd
Rounds6, with 2 in the gun
Target3×5" card above an 8" circle
StandardUnder 10 s intermediate, under 7 s advanced, under 5 s twice for the coin

From concealment: two shots on the card, slide-lock reload, four shots on the circle. Add 2 seconds for each miss on the card and 1 for each miss on the circle. Designed by Todd Green of pistol-training.com to test the draw, a precise shot, a reload, and fast accurate shots in one short run.

Garand Thumb: The MARSOC drill0:00

El Presidente

Distance10 yd
Rounds12
Target3 targets, 1 yd apart
Standard10 s good, 6 s advanced, all A's

Start facing away with hands above the shoulders. On the signal, turn, draw, fire two on each target, reload, and fire two on each again. Created by Jeff Cooper. It covers the turn, the draw, transitions between targets, and a reload under time.

Casino drill

Distance5 yd
Rounds21, 7 per magazine
TargetSix numbered shapes
Standard21 s, +1 s per miss or wrong target

One round on shape 1, two on shape 2, up to six on shape 6, with two forced reloads along the way. Adds thinking to the shooting: you have to count and pick the right target while running the gun.

Garand Thumb: The collateral drill: transitions and cadence1:17

Dot Torture and 10-10-10

Distance3 yd / 10 yd
Rounds50 / 10
Target2" dots / B-8 bullseye
Standard50 of 50 / all in the black in 10 s

Dot Torture covers draws, pairs, one-handed shooting, and transitions on ten 2-inch dots at slow speed; it's untimed and shows trigger problems clearly. The 10-10-10 is ten shots at ten yards in ten seconds from the holster on a B-8 bullseye. Both reward a clean trigger press over speed.

The Handgun and Defensive Handgun pages have more, including the 5×5, doubles, the Blake drill, and the FBI qualification.

03Rifle drills

Rifle Mozambique

Distance7–25 yd
Rounds3
Target8" chest, 3×5" head
Standard2.0 s from low ready at 7 yd; 3.0 s at 25 yd

The same two-and-one as the pistol drill, from low ready. With an optic and an AR, the head shot is where mechanical offset shows up: aim slightly high at close distances.

Ready-up

Distance25–50 yd
Rounds1
Target3×5" card
Standard1.5 s at 25 yd, 2.0 s at 50 yd

From low ready, mount the rifle and fire one precise shot. The mount, cheek weld, and first sight picture decide almost every rifle engagement, and this drill isolates them.

Dirty Civilian: Shooting standards: cold start0:59

Rifle Bill drill

Distance10–25 yd
Rounds6
Target8" circle
Standard2.5 s at 25 yd

Six shots from low ready, as fast as you can keep them in the circle. Teaches you to drive the rifle with the support hand and keep the dot in the circle through recoil.

Rifle to pistol transition

Distance7 yd
Rounds1 + 2
Target8" circle
StandardUnder 3.0 s from the click

Load the rifle with one round. Fire it, get a click on the next trigger press, let the rifle hang on the sling, draw, and fire two pistol rounds. It only makes sense inside handgun distance; farther out, fixing the rifle is usually faster.

Dirty Civilian: Shooting standards: rifle-to-pistol transition12:27

9-hole barricade

Distance50–100 yd
Rounds9–18
Target8–10" steel
StandardOne hit per hole, clean, then add time

Kyle Lamb's VTAC barricade has nine openings at different heights and angles. Fire one or two rounds through each, from the high standing hole down to prone. It builds stable improvised positions and teaches mechanical offset, because the bullet leaves below the optic and can hit the barricade edge.

Positional and KD

Distance100–500 yd
Rounds8–20
Target8–12" plates
StandardFirst-round hits from your data

Two rounds each from prone, sitting, kneeling, and standing at 100 yd; then plates at known distances using your data book. See the Rifle and Bolt Rifle pages for full standards.

04Shotgun drills

Pattern check

Distance3, 7, 15, 25 yd
Rounds4+
TargetLarge paper
StandardKnow every pellet's landing

One round of your defensive load at each distance. Measure the spread. Every other shotgun drill depends on knowing at what distance your pattern stops fitting inside the target.

Garand Thumb: How far are shotguns deadly?3:20

Shoot one, load one

Distance7 yd
Rounds5+
Target8" circle
StandardUnder 4 s per fire-load cycle

Fire one, load one shell from a sidesaddle or belt, fire again. Repeat. The gun never runs dry, and you learn to feed it in pauses rather than when it's empty.

Garand Thumb: Pump shotgun operation and reloading20:05

Shoot two, load two

Distance7 yd
Rounds4+
Target8" circle
StandardUnder 6 s per cycle

Two rounds, running the pump hard between them, then two shells into the tube. Builds the rhythm of a pump gun: fire, pump, fire, feed.

Emergency load

Distance7 yd
Rounds1
Target8" circle
Standard3.0 s from empty

Gun empty with the action open. Drop a shell through the ejection port (port load), close the action, and fire. This is how you get a pump shotgun back into action fastest when it runs dry.

Slug select

Distance25 yd
Rounds1 slug
Target6" plate
Standard5.0 s

Buckshot chambered. Switch to a slug and hit the plate. Most methods put the slug in the chamber by ejecting the chambered shell or by port loading. Used when the pattern is too wide for the distance or the target needs precision.

Kick the can

Distance7 yd
Rounds4–8
Target2+ aluminum cans
StandardTwo hits on each can while it's still moving

Paper never moves. Hitting each can, then hitting it again as it tumbles, forces you to drive the gun onto a moving target and run the action fast. Use birdshot, and only on ranges that allow it.

05Building a session

BlockTimeWhat to do
Cold test2 minOne benchmark drill, first rounds of the day. Log it.
Fundamentals15 minSlow, precise work: Dot Torture, groups, or a ready-up at distance.
Skill block20 minOne skill: draws, reloads, transitions, or movement. Short reps with a timer.
Stress test10 minA timed standard such as FAST, El Presidente, or the Casino drill.
Close5 minRepeat the cold test and compare. Write down one thing to fix next time.
